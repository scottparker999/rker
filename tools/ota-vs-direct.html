Where the money goes

Method

Payment is a settlement mix, not a single rate. OTA bookings are largely settled by virtual card, and those cards are issued on foreign BINs as commercial cards — so they reach your acquirer as cross-border commercial transactions at around 3–3.5%. A domestic guest paying on their own consumer card can be under 0.5%. On a two-night $240 booking that gap alone is roughly fifteen dollars, and it never appears in a commission comparison. Card rates here are global because they come from your merchant agreement; only the mix changes by channel.

Commission is charged on the discounted rate, not the rate you loaded. Genius, member rates, mobile rates and campaign deals come off first and stack multiplicatively, then commission applies to what is left. The discount is a real cost even though it never appears on an invoice, so it is shown as its own line.

Advertising is sunk; commission is not. This is the distinction most comparisons miss. If a booking cancels you are refunded the commission, the card fee and the platform fee — but the sponsored ad, Visibility Booster or metasearch click that won it is already spent. A channel with a high cancellation rate therefore carries its acquisition cost several times over for every booking that sticks.

A cancellation is not automatically a lost room. Cancelled nights may resell, so the resale rate and the rate you recover at are modelled rather than assuming the revenue evaporates. Resold rooms are assumed to resell through the same channel's cost structure, which is a simplification.

The breakeven direct rate is the headline output: the rate at which your direct channel exactly matches the best OTA once every cost above is counted. The gap between it and your current direct rate is what you can afford to give away — as a discount, a loyalty benefit, breakfast, parking, or ad spend to win the booking — and still be ahead. It is a ceiling on the incentive, not a target.

Keep length of stay equal unless you mean to vary it. Every figure here is per booking, so nights are part of the total. Direct bookings often do run longer, and modelling that is legitimate — but if you give one channel more nights, the per-booking gap and the breakeven headroom will reflect the extra night as well as the lower cost. Net ADR is the line to read for a like-for-like rate comparison.

Servicing cost per occupied room is deliberately excluded. It is the same whoever sends the guest, and this is a comparison of what it costs to acquire a booking, not what it costs to deliver one. For the delivery side, see the group displacement calculator.

What this does not model: repeat and lifetime value, data ownership, rate parity pressure, the billboard effect, the operational cost of virtual cards that fail to authorise or fall outside their activation window, and contracted override or override bonus structures. Several of those favour direct and would widen the gap. They are judgement, and a calculator that invents numbers for them is worse than one that leaves the room for them.
