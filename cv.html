What I do

I'm a systems specialist in hospitality and tourism. In practice that means being the person who sits between operations and technology: translating what a business actually needs into what the systems can actually do, and then making them do it.

The work runs from selecting and implementing platforms, to building the reporting layer on top of them, to writing the tools that fill the gaps vendors leave behind.

The stack

Full-stack in the hospitality sense — not front-end and back-end, but front desk to boardroom. Property management through to the reporting an executive team reads on a Monday morning.

PMS RMS Business Intelligence POS Distribution Channel management Web CRM & email Integrations & APIs

Integration

The part I'm most often called in for. Hospitality stacks are rarely one product — they're six, from five vendors, joined by connectors of varying honesty. I plan those joins, test what the documentation claims, and build the reconciliation that proves the numbers agree once the data has moved.

How I work

Operator first. I've spent long enough on the operations side to know that a system nobody uses correctly is worse than no system at all. Adoption is a design problem, not a training problem.

Build the small thing. Where a platform falls short, I'd rather ship a focused tool this week than wait two quarters for a roadmap item. A lot of my output is exactly that — self-contained dashboards, calculators and utilities that solve one problem properly.

Trust the data or fix the data. Reporting built on unreliable capture just launders bad information into confident charts.
